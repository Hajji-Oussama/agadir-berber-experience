Every city has a heartbeat. In Agadir, that heartbeat is **Kasbat Souss** — a sprawling artisan complex where the Amazigh (Berber) heritage of the Souss-Massa region lives, breathes, and works with its hands.

Most visitors come to Agadir for the beach and leave without ever seeing this side of the city. That's a mistake we want to save you from. Kasbat Souss is not a museum behind glass — it's a living workshop quarter where woodworkers, potters, leather craftsmen, jewellers and carpet weavers still work exactly as their families have for generations. And it's where our own basecamp is situated.

Here's everything you need to know before you go.

## A Brief History of Kasbat Souss

The Souss Valley has been a crossroads of Amazigh culture for over a thousand years. The region's artisans developed their crafts around the kasbahs (fortified quarters) that protected the trade routes between the Sahara, the Atlas Mountains and the Atlantic coast.

Kasbat Souss was conceived as a modern continuation of that tradition: a dedicated complex where the region's master craftsmen could keep their trades alive, pass them to new generations, and welcome visitors into their workshops. Today it houses dozens of artisan ateliers under one roof — leather, wood, metal, pottery, textiles and jewellery — each one a small world of its own.

## A Living Museum of Amazigh Crafts

Walking through Kasbat Souss is a sensory overload in the best way. Here's what you'll find in each quarter:

**Woodwork.** Master carvers shape thuya and cedar into boxes, plates and decorative pieces, the scent of the wood following you through the atelier. Watch a plain block become an intricate carved treasure in front of your eyes.

**Pottery.** Potters spin the region's clay on traditional wheels, decorating their pieces with the geometric Amazigh patterns you'll see echoed in carpets, tiles and jewellery. Many workshops invite you to try your own hand.

**Leather.** The tanners and leather workers produce bags, babouche slippers and belts with techniques unchanged for centuries. The smell of good leather — and the quality — is unmistakable.

**Jewellery.** Amazigh silversmiths create the ornate silver pieces worn by Berber women for generations: bold, geometric, and unmistakably local.

**Carpets & Textiles.** Weavers work the looms producing the famous Berber rugs, each pattern a silent language passed down through families.

**Argan & Local Produce.** Throughout the complex you'll find argan oil pressed the traditional way — worth taking home if you buy from the artisans' own shops.

## The Architecture: A Fortified Quarter in the Modern City

Kasbat Souss was built to echo the architecture of a traditional Moroccan kasbah: thick earthen walls, carved wooden doors, arched passageways and shaded courtyards that keep the heat at bay. It's a deliberately slow, human-scale space in a modern city — you'll naturally slow down, sit for tea, and let the morning disappear.

## Mint Tea & Moroccan Gastronomy

You cannot spend an hour at Kasbat Souss without being offered mint tea. It's not a drink here — it's the ritual at the heart of Amazigh hospitality. Pouring from a height, three cups, the golden stream catching the light: it's a small ceremony that makes every visit feel personal.

For a full taste of the region, the artisans' quarter sits steps away from traditional eateries serving tagine, couscous and grilled meats. Many of our guests pair a cooking class at Kasbat Souss with a visit to the workshops — the perfect half-day.

## Frequently Asked Questions

**Is Kasbat Souss free to visit?**
Yes — entering the complex and wandering the workshops is free. You only pay if you buy something, take a workshop, or book an experience.

**How long should I spend there?**
A quick wander with a glass of tea takes about an hour. Add a workshop or a meal and you'll happily spend half a day.

**Can I buy authentic souvenirs there?**
It's one of the best places in Agadir for genuine, handmade Amazigh crafts — and buying directly from the artisans supports their families and traditions.

**How do I get there?**
A short taxi ride from anywhere in Agadir (about 15 minutes from the marina). Our experiences include pickup from your hotel.
