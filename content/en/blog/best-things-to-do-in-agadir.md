You've booked the flights, checked the weather forecast (spoiler: it's going to be sunny), and now you're staring at your screen wondering what to actually **do** with your week in Agadir. That's exactly why we're writing this.

We've been guiding travelers around this corner of Morocco for years — through the dunes, the forests, the souks, and the hidden beaches the brochures never show. So we're going to tell you, straight and without the tourist-marketing sugarcoat: where to go, what to skip, and what you should expect to pay.

Agadir gets a weird reputation as "just a beach town with hotels." People who say that never left the hotel strip. The real Agadir — the one we take our guests to — starts about twenty minutes from your sunbed. It's quad tracks across dunes that glow gold at six in the evening. It's Berber villages where the tagine has been bubbling over charcoal since dawn. It's a souk so huge you'll get lost twice, on purpose.

And then there's the weather. Three hundred-plus sunny days a year. In January, we're in T-shirts while your friends back home are scraping ice off their windscreens. In August, the Atlantic breeze keeps the beach bearable while Marrakech bakes forty kilometres inland.

Enough introduction. Here's our honest top 10, the scams to dodge, and the questions we get asked every single day.

## 1. Ride the Desert Dunes on a Quad Bike

If you only book one thing in Agadir, make it this.

We pick you up from your hotel, drive you out past the city limits to the coastal dunes, and hand you the handlebars of a quad bike that's actually looked after (important detail — plenty of operators run beat-up machines; we don't). Then you're off: two hours of tearing across golden sand, climbing dune faces that look impossible until you commit, and cruising the beach line where the desert literally meets the Atlantic.

The first ten minutes are nervous. Everybody's nervous. By minute twenty you'll be grinning like a kid who just discovered what a throttle does. By the end, you'll be asking us if we can do it again tomorrow.

**Practical bits:**
- Duration: about 2 hours including transport
- Price: **400 MAD** (roughly €35–40)
- Best time: **late afternoon** — the light is unreal and the heat drops
- What to wear: closed shoes, sunglasses, and a scarf or buff for the dust (we provide the helmet)

A quick note: a **sunset slot** books out fast, so don't leave it to your last day.

## 2. Watch the Sunset from the Kasbah Ruins

Every travel blog tells you to visit "Agadir Oufella" — the hilltop kasbah. Here's the honest version: the kasbah itself is a ruined fortress with not much left except walls, a café, and a lot of history. What's not a cliché is the **view**.

You stand up there at golden hour, the whole bay curving below you, the medina rebuilt flat and white after the 1960 earthquake, the port working in the distance, and the sun dropping straight into the Atlantic. It's the single best photo of Agadir you'll take — and it's free.

**Practical bits:**
- How to get there: it's a steep 20-minute walk up, or grab a petit taxi (about 20 MAD from the centre)
- Go **45 minutes before sunset**, grab a mint tea at the café, and claim a spot on the outer wall
- Bring a jacket even in summer — the breeze up top is strong

One tip: ignore anyone who tells you there's an "entrance fee" or that they're the official guide. There isn't and they aren't.

## 3. Get Lost in Souk El Had

Souk El Had is one of the largest markets in Africa — and yes, it's that big. 6,000 shops under one roof, a maze of alleyways selling spices, leather, carpets, argan oil, jewellery, and some things we genuinely don't know what they are.

Here's how to do it like a local:

- **Go in the morning**, when the stalls are fresh and the air is cool.
- **Don't buy argan oil from the first stall you see.** Compare prices. The difference between stall one and stall twelve can be double.
- **Haggling is not a battle, it's a conversation.** Offer about half the asking price, meet somewhere in the middle, and smile the whole time. If you're not enjoying it, you're doing it wrong.
- **Cash is king here.** Small notes, please.
- The spice section smells incredible. Buy saffron from the shops that display it loose, not pre-packaged "tourist saffron" in a fancy box.

You'll get lost. That's part of the fun. Eventually you'll find the exit — or we'll find you.

## 4. Horse Riding Along the Beach at Golden Hour

There are a few places on earth where you can ride a horse on the sand with the sun setting over the ocean. Agadir is one of them, and honestly, it never gets old for us either.

We take you through the Aghroud forest first — cork oaks, sandy trails, the silence you only get away from the city — and then you break out onto the beach and open up the pace. If you've never ridden, our horses are the calm, "I've done this a thousand times" kind. If you have ridden, you'll get to actually move.

At the end, there's mint tea and often a barbecue waiting back at our base. It sounds like a cliché until you're actually sitting there, sandy, tired, and holding a glass of sweet tea you didn't know you needed.

**Practical bits:**
- Duration: about 2 hours
- Price: **350 MAD**
- No experience needed — we match the horse to you
- This is the number one **couples** activity we run. Bring your camera.

## 5. Camel Trekking Through the Aghroud Forest

Camel trekking has a reputation for being a "tourist thing." True — and sometimes it is. But a slow ride through the ancient Aghroud forest, past the Souss River, with mint tea and a barbecue at the end of it? That's not a box-ticking exercise, that's an afternoon.

The camels are gentle, patient, and frankly a bit dramatic about standing up and sitting down (you'll understand the first time). You'll get photos that make your friends at home ask questions. And unlike the desert-safari camps two hours away, this one is twenty minutes from your hotel — which means you're not spending your whole holiday in a minibus.

**Practical bits:**
- Duration: about 2 hours
- Price: **300 MAD**
- Perfect for families — the pace is slow and the animals are bomb-proof
- Slightly cooler in the forest, so it works well on hot afternoons

## 6. Take a Buggy Off-Road Into the Desert

## 7. Learn to Cook Tagine Like a Moroccan

A cooking class in Agadir is not a demonstration where you watch and clap. It's a proper working session: you'll walk through the market to pick your own vegetables, then stand at a stove and make tagine and couscous the way your grandmother's Moroccan equivalent makes it — from feel, not from a recipe.

The teachers are real home cooks, not actors in a kitchen set. The food at the end is the best meal of your trip, and it's one you made yourself. It also gives you a skill you'll take home and annoy your friends with.

**Practical bits:**
- Allow about 4–5 hours including the market visit and eating
- You'll eat what you cook — come hungry
- Great for couples and families alike; kids get to roll the couscous with their hands (this is a highlight, trust us)

## 8. Surf and Unwind in Taghazout

Taghazout is a twenty-minute drive north — a little surf town that went from fishing village to backpacker magnet without losing its soul. The beach is fine sand, the waves are consistent, and the cafés serve the best flat white you'll find in the whole region.

Even if you've never surfed, this is worth the trip. Rent a board for 100–150 MAD an hour, take a lesson if you want, or just sit on the cliffs and watch the local crew catch waves. The sunset over the surf line is a legitimately great way to spend an evening.

**How to get there:** local bus (about 15 MAD), grand taxi, or just include it in a day out with a driver. It's not worth hiring a car for.

## 9. Escape to Paradise Valley

If you want to trade the city for green pools, rock pools, and a deep canyon, Paradise Valley is your day trip. It's about an hour and a half inland — a series of natural swimming spots in the Argan forest where the water is shockingly cold and absolutely clear.

**Honest advice from us:**
- **Go early** — by 11am it's busy, and by summer it's busy-times-ten.
- Bring proper footwear. The rocks are slippery and hot.
- There's no real infrastructure — no showers, limited shade, and the "cafés" are basic. That's the charm.
- If you're with us on a private trip, we'll take you to the quieter upper pools the tour vans miss.

It's not the Sahara. It's better — it's an hour away and you'll be back in Agadir in time for dinner.

## 10. Eat Fish Like a Local at the Marina

Agadir's marina is where the boats come in, and the fish restaurants on the waterfront grill the morning's catch within hours of it being landed. You don't need a reservation and you don't need to dress up — you need to be hungry.

**How to order without getting overcharged:**
- Look for the fish displayed on ice **at the front** of the restaurant — that's fresh.
- Ask the price **per kilo** before you point at anything. This is the single most important sentence in Moroccan seafood dining.
- A whole grilled sea bass for two, with salad and bread, should land you around 200–300 MAD depending on the fish. If you're quoted double that, walk.
- The sardines (yes, sardines) grilled with chermoula are the secret local order. Cheap, incredible, and completely missed by tourists.

## Local Tips: How to Avoid Tourist Traps & Scams in Agadir

Agadir is safe and genuinely friendly — but "safe" and "free of overcharging" are two different things. Here's what we tell our own guests, family, and friends before they explore alone.

**Taxis: the number one trap.** There are two kinds of taxi: the *petit taxi* (small, beige, city only) and the *grand taxi* (bigger, for longer trips). Rules:
- Petit taxis have a meter, but drivers often "forget" it. **Say "compteur" (counter) before you get in.** If they refuse, get out and take the next one.
- For short trips (1–3 km), 20–30 MAD is fair. From the airport to the centre is a fixed rate of roughly 250 MAD — agree it **before** you sit down.
- There's no Uber in Agadir (there's "Heetch", but don't rely on it). Cash is your backup.

**Fake guides.** Around the Kasbah, the Souk, and the Marina, men will approach with "my friend, I show you the real Agadir, no money." It always becomes money. A polite "la shukran" (no thank you) repeated with a smile is all you need. If someone insists you need a guide to enter a site, they're lying.

**The free "gift" at shops.** If someone ties a bracelet on your wrist or presses a scarf into your hands, it's a sales tactic, not generosity. Hand it back, smile, keep walking.

**Souk El Had haggling done right.** The opening price is always inflated. Offer 40–50% of it, and expect to settle around 60–70% of the original ask. And here's the pro move: **walk away**. If they call you back, you've hit the real price. If they don't, you weren't going to get it anyway.

**ATM and card reality.** Big hotels and restaurants take cards. Almost everything else — taxis, souks, small cafés, most experiences — runs on cash. Don't assume you can pay by card and don't rely on one ATM; the machines near the marina have long queues in season.

**Photography.** Moroccans are famously generous about being photographed — but ask first, always. For shopkeepers, it's normal to buy something small after a photo. For women, be especially respectful and never photograph without asking.

**The "it's closed today" trick.** At certain sights, you'll be told the site or road is "closed today — but my brother has a shop/ride/driver." It's not closed. Walk on.

## FAQ

**Is Agadir safe for tourists?**

Yes — Agadir is one of the safest cities in Morocco for visitors. Violent crime against tourists is extremely rare, and the city is full of families and locals going about their day. The real risks are petty ones: overcharging, pickpocketing in the crowded souk, and the scams listed above. Keep your phone out of your back pocket in the Souk, use the taxi tips above, and you'll have zero problems. For solo female travelers, the main annoyance is persistent street sellers — a confident "no" repeated firmly usually ends it.

**Do I need cash or card in Agadir?**

Both — and the balance matters. Bring a card for your hotel, nicer restaurants, and any big bookings (like our experiences, which you can book online). But plan for **cash** as your daily default: taxis, the souk, beach cafés, street food, and most excursion operators are cash-only. You can withdraw at bank ATMs (they're everywhere), and Moroccan dirhams — MAD — are what you need. Exchange at a bank or official exchange office, not at "great rates" offered by strangers.

**Can I drink the tap water in Agadir?**

No. Drink bottled water, which is cheap and sold everywhere (5–10 MAD for 1.5L). That includes brushing your teeth if you have a sensitive stomach. Ice in tourist restaurants and hotels is generally made from filtered water and fine to use — but street-side ice from unknown sources is a gamble. And skip the "free water with the mint tea" unless you saw the bottle opened.

**What's the best time of year to visit Agadir?**

Agadir is genuinely a year-round destination, but here's the honest breakdown:
- **October to May** — the sweet spot. 20–28°C, sunny, and the "winter" months are mild enough for beach days.
- **March to May** is our personal favourite: warm days, green countryside, and before the summer crowds.
- **June to September** — hot (30–38°C), busy, and pricier, but the ocean breeze keeps the beach livable.
- December and January are the coolest but still pleasant in the sun; evenings need a light jacket.
